# Write Concisely

Use this skill when drafting or revising prose for people. Preserve the meaning, remove
friction, and make the next action obvious.

COL-331 selected NeoLabHQ's
[write-concisely skill](https://github.com/NeoLabHQ/context-engineering-kit/blob/master/plugins/docs/skills/write-concisely/SKILL.md)
as the intake reference. This project version independently applies the same
public-domain principles from William Strunk Jr.'s *The Elements of Style* (1918)
through a shorter workflow, modern caveats, and revision checks.

## Method

1. Identify the document's reader and purpose.
2. Put the conclusion or required action first.
3. Give each paragraph one topic and begin with its topic sentence.
4. Replace vague, passive, or indirect wording with concrete language.
5. Remove every word, sentence, and section that does not help the reader act or understand.
6. Read the revision once for meaning and once for rhythm.

## Core rules

### Structure

- Give one paragraph to each topic.
- Begin each paragraph with a topic sentence.
- Keep related words together.
- Express coordinate ideas in parallel form.
- Keep one tense in summaries.
- Put the most important words at the end of a sentence when that improves emphasis.

### Sentences

- Prefer active voice when the actor matters.
- State what is true instead of only what is not true.
- Use definite, specific, concrete language.
- Join independent clauses with a conjunction, semicolon, or period—not a comma alone.
- Do not split one complete sentence into fragments.
- Ensure an opening participial phrase modifies the sentence's grammatical subject.
- Vary sentence structure when repeated loose sentences become monotonous.

### Punctuation

- Form a singular possessive by adding `'s` unless a house style requires otherwise.
- Use the serial comma unless a house style requires otherwise.
- Enclose parenthetic expressions with matching punctuation.
- Put a comma before a conjunction that joins two independent clauses.

## Omit needless words

Prefer the shorter form when meaning stays intact:

| Wordy | Concise |
| --- | --- |
| owing to the fact that | because |
| in spite of the fact that | although |
| the question as to whether | whether |
| call your attention to | show |
| in a hasty manner | hastily |
| he is a person who | he |
| did not remember | forgot |
| did not pay attention to | ignored |

Delete throat-clearing such as:

- “It is interesting to note that”
- “It should be mentioned that”
- “In order to”
- “There is” or “There are” when a concrete subject can lead
- Repeated summaries of what the document already showed

## Revision checks

Before finalizing, verify:

- The first sentence states the conclusion, request, or purpose.
- Each paragraph supports one idea.
- Every pronoun has an unambiguous antecedent.
- Every list uses parallel grammar.
- Concrete nouns and verbs replace vague abstractions where possible.
- Passive voice remains only when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or deliberately
  de-emphasized.
- No deletion changed a requirement, qualification, warning, or technical fact.

Concise writing is not uniformly short writing. Keep examples, evidence, caveats, and
context that the reader needs; remove only what does not earn its place.
